Copy never breaks a date

In the editor a date is one piece, a chip. Every way out of the editor writes it as normal text, and every way back in turns it into a chip again. Nothing is lost, whichever app you paste into.

In Blade Notes · select, then ⌘C
• Book the renewal appointment Sun 15 Nov · 10:00
One chip = one event in the calendar.
Paste into Gmail, Google Docs, Slack
• Book the renewal appointment — Sun 15 Nov 2026, 10:00
Rich copy: the date is real text inside a <time> tag that also carries the exact moment.
Paste into WhatsApp, a terminal, any plain box
- Book the renewal appointment — Sun 15 Nov 2026, 10:00
Plain copy writes the date in words. Without this, a custom piece copies as an empty gap.
Paste back into Blade Notes
Copy + paste: a chip again, as a new event on the same date. Deleting one never deletes the other.
Cut + paste: the same event moves; its calendar entry stays.

What one click copies

  • Title, path, status, due date and priority.
  • The whole description, every date written out.
  • Hidden descriptions, as a quote under their line.
  • Attachment names only: files stay behind your sign-in, so no public links leave the app.
  • Rich text and Markdown together; the app you paste into takes the one it understands.
  • A Secret block (security phase 2) copies as •••• unless you reveal it first.

How the chip makes this work

  • Plain text: the chip writes itself as Sun 15 Nov 2026, 10:00.
  • Rich text: it writes <time datetime="2026-11-15T10:00+06:00"> around the same words.
  • Paste in: a <time> from Blade Notes becomes a chip again; if its event is still in a note, it gets a new id.
  • Description links get the same rule. Today a copied phrase keeps pointing at the first note’s description.
